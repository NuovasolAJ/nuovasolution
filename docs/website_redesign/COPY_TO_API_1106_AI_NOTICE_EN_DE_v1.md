## 4. What counsel should see

A faithful translation carries over what the source says, so these points sit in the Spanish too. I decide none of them.

| # | Point | Where it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Spanish e-mail text mixes forms of address:** "Si deseas … indíquelo en su respuesta" (tú, then usted). EN has no such choice; DE uses Sie throughout. Here the translation is deliberately not word for word. | ES v1.0, e-mail |
| 2 | **German form of address on WhatsApp:** faithful to "te" and "prefieres" is **du**. Agencies in German-speaking markets usually write to property customers with **Sie**, and the notice has to match the reply in the same chat. See the line in §6. | DE, WhatsApp |
| 3 | **"Agente humano" / "ask for a person":** on the phone the owner's text dropped this because there is no transfer. In WhatsApp and e-mail it means that a person takes over the reply. Is that enough? | WhatsApp, e-mail |
| 4 | **The v1.1 e-mail says "asistente digital", not "inteligencia artificial".** Does that meet the duty? And "con la supervisión de nuestro equipo" is a statement of fact: it holds only if a team really supervises. | v1.1, all languages |
| 5 | **"Sistema automático":** `v2.0-es-voice` said it, `v3.0-es-voice` does not. The translations follow v3.0. | Phone |
| 6 | **"KI" in German** instead of "künstliche Intelligenz": short and common, and the Spanish spells it out. If counsel wants it spelled out: "ein Assistent mit künstlicher Intelligenz". | DE, all channels |
| 7 | **Changing language in a conversation** (owner `SPRACHWECHSEL = JA`): is the notice repeated in the new language? My proposal: yes, once, with the first automatic reply in that language, because the customer may not have understood the first one. | all channels |
